# How STME Works

STME transforms a decision problem into a structured space of states and transitions.

It does not solve the decision.

It makes the structure of the decision visible.

---

## Step 1 — State Decomposition

A user question is decomposed into multiple concurrent states.

Each state represents a factor within the decision space.

States are classified into three perspectives:

- **Internal** — personal goals, values, satisfaction
- **External** — environment, market, external conditions
- **Structural** — risks, constraints, irreversible factors

Each state also includes attributes:

- **Role** (Core / Functional)
- **Perspective** (Internal / External)
- **Dynamics** (Active / Static)
- **Validity** (Real / Potential)

---

## Step 2 — State Preservation

Unlike traditional systems, STME does not collapse states into a single outcome.

Multiple states are preserved simultaneously, even if they conflict.

This allows:

- uncertainty to remain visible
- contradictions to coexist
- incomplete information to be retained

---

## Step 3 — Structural Analysis

STME analyzes the overall decision structure:

- Core vs Functional balance
- Internal vs External pressure
- Active vs Static dynamics
- Real vs Potential grounding

This provides a structural snapshot of the decision space.

---

## Step 4 — Transition Modeling

Transitions represent possible movements between states.

Each transition includes:

- origin state
- target direction (e.g. potential → real, static → active)
- cost
- time
- controllability
- impact

---

## Step 5 — Transition Evaluation

Transitions are evaluated using a structural scoring function:

Score = (Impact × Controllability) / (Cost × Time)

This does not determine the correct action.

It identifies which transitions are structurally more feasible.

---

## Step 6 — Ranking Without Collapse

Transitions are ranked by priority.

However:

- no final decision is selected
- no state is eliminated
- no forced convergence occurs

The decision space remains open.

---

## Step 7 — Interpretation Layer

STME results can be interpreted to reveal:

- where pressure exists
- what is controllable vs external
- which paths are structurally viable
- where uncertainty remains

---

## Key Principle

STME does not optimize for answers.

It preserves structure long enough for meaningful decisions to emerge.

---

## One-Line Summary

STME does not decide.

It prevents decisions from collapsing too early.
