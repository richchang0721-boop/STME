# What is STME?

STME (Structured Multi-State Transition & Evaluation) is a framework designed to make decision structures observable under uncertainty.

It does not provide answers.

Instead, it preserves multiple possible states within a decision space, identifies structural pressures, and evaluates possible transitions without forcing premature convergence.

---

## Why STME Exists

Most AI systems are optimized to produce a single answer.

However, real-world decisions do not naturally collapse into a single outcome.

They exist as:

- competing possibilities
- partial knowledge
- conflicting constraints
- evolving conditions

The problem is not the absence of answers.

The problem is premature closure.

---

## Core Idea

STME is built on a simple principle:

> A decision should not collapse before its structure is understood.

Instead of selecting an answer, STME:

- represents multiple concurrent states
- preserves uncertainty
- highlights structural constraints
- ranks transitions between states

---

## What STME Is Not

STME is not:

- a recommendation engine
- a prediction model
- a decision-making AI

It does not tell users what to do.

---

## What STME Is

STME is:

- a structural decision framework
- a state-preserving reasoning system
- a way to expose hidden pressures before decisions are made

---

## Relationship to PIDA and CIP

STME is part of a broader framework:

- **CIP (Cognitive Integrity Protocol)**  
  Defines the boundaries of cognitive autonomy.

- **PIDA (Pre-Incident Responsibility Architecture)**  
  Defines how interaction with AI should be structured.

- **STME**  
  Operationalizes decision structure within those boundaries.

---

## One-Line Summary

STME does not give you the answer.

It shows you what remains possible — and what is already constrained.
